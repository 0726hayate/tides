Tides is an offline-first period tracker designed for users who want
their cycle data to stay on their device — no accounts, no analytics,
no cloud sync.

The app's manifest does not declare the INTERNET permission. Tides
cannot make a network call even if a future bug tried to.

What you get:

* Calendar view with cycle day, period days, and fertile-window
  indicators (color-blind-safe glyphs throughout).
* Two-tap daily logging: flow intensity, symptoms from a curated
  taxonomy, optional note.
* Cycle and period statistics with FIGO-aligned regularity descriptors,
  symptom heatmaps, and monthly insight cards built from your own data.
* PDF and CSV export, including a doctor-formatted summary that follows
  the FIGO System 1 menstrual descriptors used in clinical settings.
* Encrypted backup and restore with a separate backup password.
* Optional period reminders (off by default, scheduled locally — Tides
  has no internet permission, so reminders run entirely on device).
* PIN unlock with optional biometric. The encryption key is derived
  from your PIN via Argon2id; biometric unlock unwraps a key bound
  to the Android Keystore.
* Optional duress PIN that opens a clean decoy database.

Privacy and threat model:

* All cycle data lives in a single SQLCipher-encrypted database.
* No telemetry, no crash reporting, no analytics, no A/B testing,
  no remote config. None of these are present in the build.
* Three privacy presets at onboarding (Just for me, Locked when away,
  Always locked) tune lock timeouts, notification visibility, and
  duress-PIN availability.

Tides is free software under GPL-3.0. There are no in-app purchases.
A Ko-fi link is available in Settings for users who want to support
development, but the app itself never asks for money.
