# Menstrual Cycle Distribution Research — Reference for Persona Test Suite

Survey of population-level menstrual cycle statistics and individual clinical case patterns for use in offline, hand-transcribed persona generation. Every numeric claim is followed by its primary citation. Where a peer-reviewed case report was not publicly accessible, an ACOG / clinical practice substitute is marked explicitly.

---

## Section 1: Statistical parameters by population segment

## Segment: Typical adult age 25-35

- **Cycle length:** Mean 29.3 days (SD ~3 days). Bull et al. (n=612,613 cycles) report mean 29.3 days; only ~13% of cycles fall within the historically assumed 27–29 day range. Modern data show cycle-length distribution is right-skewed (closer to log-normal than normal), with long-cycle tail driven by occasional anovulatory or oligo-ovulatory cycles. Source: Bull JR, Rowland SP, Scherwitzl EB et al. "Real-world menstrual cycle characteristics of more than 600,000 menstrual cycles." *npj Digital Medicine* 2019;2:83. DOI: 10.1038/s41746-019-0152-7. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-019-0152-7
- **Period length:** Mean 4.0 days, SD 1.4 days for age 25–29 and 3.9 days SD 1.5 for 30–34 (Bull et al. 2019, same DOI).
- **Anovulation rate:** ~3–8% of cycles in healthy regularly-menstruating women in this age band; up to ~12% sporadically. Source: Prior JC et al. "Ovulation Prevalence in Women with Spontaneous Normal-Length Menstrual Cycles – A Population-Based Cohort from HUNT3, Norway." *PLOS ONE* 2015;10(8):e0134473. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0134473.
- **Common symptoms (rough prevalence):** Abdominal cramps ~84%, bloating ~70%, breast tenderness ~50%, mood changes / irritability ~50%, headache ~30%, acne ~25%, fatigue ~60%. Source: Apple Women's Health Study aggregated symptom prevalence — Mahalingaiah S et al. "Menstrual cycle length variation by demographic characteristics from the Apple Women's Health Study." *npj Digital Medicine* 2023. DOI: 10.1038/s41746-023-00848-1. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-023-00848-1
- **Common bleeding-pattern abnormalities:** Heavy menstrual bleeding (HMB) prevalence ~10–30%, intermenstrual spotting ~5–10%. FIGO definitions (cycle frequency normal 24–38d, duration ≤8d, volume not heavy). Source: Munro MG et al. "The two FIGO systems for normal and abnormal uterine bleeding symptoms... 2018 revisions." *Int J Gynecol Obstet* 2018. DOI: 10.1002/ijgo.12666.

## Segment: Typical adult age 35-45

- **Cycle length:** Mean cycle length declines by ~0.18 days per year of age from 25 to 45; mean ~28.5 days at age 35, ~27 days at age 45. SD increases modestly with age; distribution remains right-skewed. Source: Bull et al. 2019, *npj Digital Medicine* 2:83. DOI: 10.1038/s41746-019-0152-7.
- **Period length:** Mean 3.8 days SD 1.5 (age 35–39); 3.7 days SD 1.5 (age 40–45). Source: Bull et al. 2019.
- **Anovulation rate:** Rises from ~5% at age 35 to ~15–20% by age 45 due to declining follicular reserve. Source: Harlow SD et al. "Executive summary of the STRAW+10 Workshop." *Fertil Steril* 2012. DOI: 10.1016/j.fertnstert.2012.01.128.
- **Common symptoms:** Similar prevalence to younger adults but with rising incidence of heavier or more variable bleeding episodes and early vasomotor symptoms appearing in late 30s/early 40s in ~10–20%. Source: SWAN Study — Gold EB et al., reviewed in Santoro N. "Perimenopause: from research to practice." *J Womens Health* 2016. DOI: 10.1089/jwh.2015.5556.
- **Common bleeding-pattern abnormalities:** Rising prevalence of heavy menstrual bleeding (HMB), structural causes (fibroids in ~20–40% by age 40, adenomyosis), and ovulatory disturbance. Source: Munro MG et al. FIGO PALM-COEIN 2018. DOI: 10.1002/ijgo.12666.

## Segment: PCOS (Rotterdam criteria)

- **Cycle length:** Markedly variable. Oligomenorrhea (cycle >35 days, <9 cycles/year) is a defining feature in ~70–80% of PCOS patients; some cycles 45–120 days, occasional secondary amenorrhea (>90 days). Distribution is heavily right-skewed and bimodal. Source: Teede HJ et al. "International evidence-based guideline for the assessment and management of polycystic ovary syndrome 2023." *Fertil Steril* 2023. DOI: 10.1016/j.fertnstert.2023.07.025. Also: Rotterdam ESHRE/ASRM-Sponsored PCOS Consensus Workshop Group. "Revised 2003 consensus on diagnostic criteria and long-term health risks related to polycystic ovary syndrome." *Fertil Steril* 2004;81:19–25.
- **Period length:** When bleeding occurs, often prolonged (>7 days) and unpredictable in volume due to unopposed estrogen-driven endometrial buildup. Source: Teede HJ et al. 2023 guideline.
- **Anovulation rate:** ~70–90% of cycles are anovulatory. Source: Azziz R et al. "The prevalence and features of the polycystic ovary syndrome in an unselected population." *J Clin Endocrinol Metab* 2004;89:2745. DOI: 10.1210/jc.2003-032046.
- **Common symptoms:** Hirsutism 65–80%, acne ~43% (vs ~21% controls), weight gain / central adiposity ~50–60%, hair thinning ~20%. Insulin resistance in 60–70%. Source: Lizneva D et al. "Criteria, prevalence, and phenotypes of polycystic ovary syndrome." *Fertil Steril* 2016;106:6. DOI: 10.1016/j.fertnstert.2016.05.003. AAFP review: https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2023/0300/polycystic-ovary-syndrome.html
- **Common bleeding-pattern abnormalities:** Oligomenorrhea, secondary amenorrhea, and intermittent heavy / prolonged anovulatory bleeding (AUB-O in PALM-COEIN). Elevated endometrial hyperplasia risk with chronic anovulation. Source: ACOG Practice Bulletin 194 "Polycystic Ovary Syndrome" 2018.

## Segment: Perimenopause (age 45-55, late reproductive stage)

- **Cycle length:** Early menopausal transition is defined by persistent ≥7-day difference between consecutive cycles. Late transition marked by skipped cycles ≥60 days. Mean cycle length first shortens (Stage -3a, late reproductive) then becomes highly variable. SD of cycle length increases sharply (often >7 days). Source: Harlow SD et al. "Executive summary of the Stages of Reproductive Aging Workshop + 10." *Fertil Steril* 2012;97:843. DOI: 10.1016/j.fertnstert.2012.01.128. https://www.fertstert.org/article/S0015-0282(12)00187-2/fulltext
- **Period length:** Variable; often longer or heavier in early transition due to anovulatory cycles with prolonged unopposed estrogen, then progressively lighter as estrogen falls. Source: Harlow SD et al. STRAW+10 2012; also Hale GE et al. "Atypical estradiol secretion and ovulation patterns caused by luteal out-of-phase events." *Menopause* 2009. DOI: 10.1097/gme.0b013e3181c89bf2.
- **Anovulation rate:** Rises to 30–60% in early transition, >70% in late transition. Source: STRAW+10 (Harlow 2012).
- **Common symptoms:** Vasomotor symptoms (hot flashes / night sweats) in up to 80% of US women across transition, average 4–5 episodes/day; sleep disturbance ~40–60%; mood changes ~20–30%; vaginal dryness rising into postmenopause. Source: Avis NE et al., SWAN, "Vasomotor symptoms across the menopause transition." *JAMA Intern Med* 2015;175:531. DOI: 10.1001/jamainternmed.2014.8063.
- **Common bleeding-pattern abnormalities:** Anovulatory bleeding (AUB-O), heavy menstrual bleeding, intermenstrual bleeding. AUB in perimenopause requires endometrial evaluation. Source: ACOG Committee Opinion 557 "Management of acute abnormal uterine bleeding in nonpregnant reproductive-aged women."

## Segment: Adolescent (age 13-18, gynecologic age 0-3)

- **Cycle length:** In first gynecologic year, 5th percentile 23 days and 95th percentile 90 days. Normal range 21–45 days (broader than adult). By gynecologic year 3, ~60–80% of cycles fall in 21–34 day range. Cycles in girls <1 year post-menarche have OR 2.6 for high variability and OR 5.0 for short cycles vs ≥6 years post-menarche. Source: Carlson LJ, Shaw ND. "Menstrual Cycle Characteristics of U.S. Adolescents According to Gynecologic Age and Age at Menarche." *J Pediatr Adolesc Gynecol* 2024. DOI: 10.1016/j.jpag.2024.04.002. Also: ACOG Committee Opinion 651 "Menstruation in Girls and Adolescents: Using the Menstrual Cycle as a Vital Sign." https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2015/12/menstruation-in-girls-and-adolescents-using-the-menstrual-cycle-as-a-vital-sign
- **Period length:** ≤7 days considered normal. Source: ACOG Committee Opinion 651.
- **Anovulation rate:** ~50–80% of cycles anovulatory in first gynecologic year; falls to ~20% by year 3–5 as HPO axis matures. Source: Apter D. "Endocrine and metabolic abnormalities in adolescents with a PCOS-like condition." *J Clin Endocrinol Metab* 1995. Reviewed in Hickey M, Balen A. *Hum Reprod Update* 2003;9:493.
- **Common symptoms:** Dysmenorrhea in ~50–90% of adolescents, mood disturbance, acne (40–50% have some facial acne in mid-adolescence). Source: ACOG Committee Opinion 760 "Dysmenorrhea and Endometriosis in the Adolescent" 2018.
- **Common bleeding-pattern abnormalities:** AUB-O common; heavy menstrual bleeding at menarche raises suspicion of inherited bleeding disorder (von Willebrand prevalence ~13% in adolescents with HMB). Source: ACOG Committee Opinion 785 "Screening and management of bleeding disorders in adolescents with heavy menstrual bleeding."

## Segment: Post-partum non-lactating

- **Cycle length:** First menses typically returns 6–8 weeks postpartum; range 4 weeks to 6 months. First few cycles often anovulatory and irregular; most return to baseline pattern by 3–6 months. Source: Jackson E, Glasier A. "Return of ovulation and menses in postpartum nonlactating women: a systematic review." *Obstet Gynecol* 2011;117:657. DOI: 10.1097/AOG.0b013e31820ce18c.
- **Period length:** Similar to pre-pregnancy after a few cycles; often heavier or longer in first 1–2 cycles. Source: Jackson E, Glasier A 2011.
- **Anovulation rate:** First postpartum cycle anovulatory in 40–70% of non-lactating women; declines steeply over subsequent cycles. Source: Jackson E, Glasier A 2011.
- **Common symptoms:** Postpartum mood symptoms (~10–20% PPD), fatigue; cycle-related symptoms re-emerge with HPO recovery.
- **Common bleeding-pattern abnormalities:** Initial heavier first menses; rule out retained products if persistent heavy bleeding beyond 6 weeks. Source: ACOG Practice Bulletin 183 "Postpartum hemorrhage."

## Segment: Post-partum lactational amenorrhea

- **Cycle length:** Amenorrhea expected during exclusive breastfeeding within first 6 months; LAM 98%+ effective when criteria met (exclusive/near-exclusive breastfeeding, amenorrhea, <6 months postpartum). Median return of menses ranges 6–12 months in exclusively breastfeeding women; up to 18+ months with continued nursing. Source: Kennedy KI, Trussell J. "Postpartum contraception and lactation," reviewed in CDC US Medical Eligibility Criteria, Appendix G. https://www.cdc.gov/contraception/hcp/usmec/lactational-amenorrhea-method.html
- **Period length:** N/A during amenorrhea; first return often heavier than baseline.
- **Anovulation rate:** Suppression of GnRH pulsatility by elevated prolactin → near-total anovulation while LAM criteria met. First menses preceded by ovulation in ~40–80% of women — i.e., ovulation can return before first observed period. Source: Campbell OM, Gray RH. "Characteristics and determinants of postpartum ovarian function in women in the United States." *Am J Obstet Gynecol* 1993;169:55. DOI: 10.1016/0002-9378(93)90131-2.
- **Common symptoms:** Breast tenderness, lactation-related symptoms; cycle symptoms minimal during amenorrhea.
- **Common bleeding-pattern abnormalities:** Sporadic spotting episodes may occur even within LAM and do not necessarily indicate fertility return (must persist ≥2 consecutive days and occur ≥56 days postpartum to count as menses per WHO LAM definition).

## Segment: Combined oral contraceptive (steady state)

- **Cycle length:** Standard 21/7 regimen → withdrawal bleed every 28 days during placebo week. 24/4 and 26/2 regimens shorten hormone-free interval. Source: ACOG Practice Bulletin 206 "Use of Hormonal Contraception in Women With Coexisting Medical Conditions" 2019; also Edelman A et al. "Continuous combination oral contraceptive pills to eliminate withdrawal bleeding: a randomized trial." *Obstet Gynecol* 2006. DOI: 10.1097/01.AOG.0000218889.95066.0e.
- **Period length:** Withdrawal bleed typically 3–5 days (range 2–7), generally lighter than natural menses. Source: WHO Selected Practice Recommendations for Contraceptive Use, 3rd ed. 2016.
- **Anovulation rate:** ~100% by design (gonadotropin suppression); occasional ovulation with missed pills. Source: WHO SPR 2016.
- **Common symptoms:** Breast tenderness ~20%, nausea ~10–20% (mostly first 1–3 months), mood changes ~5–15%, headache ~10%. Source: ACOG Practice Bulletin 206 2019.
- **Common bleeding-pattern abnormalities:** Breakthrough bleeding/spotting in 10–30% in first 3 months, declining to <10% by month 6. Persistent BTB after 3 months → consider higher-dose or different progestin. Source: WHO SPR 2016.

## Segment: Hormonal IUD — first 3 months (adjustment phase)

- **Cycle length:** Irregular; frequent bleeding/spotting days. Mean 16–20 bleeding/spotting days per 90-day reference period in months 1–3 of Mirena. Source: Mirena prescribing information, Bayer. https://labeling.bayerhealthcare.com/html/products/pi/Mirena_PI.pdf
- **Period length:** Cumulative spotting / bleeding days high; discrete "periods" not well defined. Median 8–10 bleeding days in first 90 days. Source: Hidalgo M et al. "Bleeding patterns and clinical performance of the levonorgestrel-releasing intrauterine system (Mirena) up to two years." *Contraception* 2002;65:129. DOI: 10.1016/s0010-7824(01)00302-x.
- **Anovulation rate:** Mirena suppresses ovulation in ~25% of cycles in steady state; in first 3 months ovulation often continues but endometrium thins. Source: Nilsson CG et al., reviewed in Mirena PI.
- **Common symptoms:** Cramping (especially first weeks post-insertion), spotting, mood changes (~5%), acne (~5–15%), breast tenderness, headache (~16%). Source: Mirena PI 2022.
- **Common bleeding-pattern abnormalities:** Unscheduled bleeding in 31.9% of users (any time during use, peak in first 6 months). Source: Mirena PI 2022.

## Segment: Hormonal IUD — steady state amenorrhea (Mirena ~20% prevalence)

- **Cycle length:** No bleeding; "cycle length" undefined. ~20% of Mirena users amenorrheic at 1 year; ~34% by year 8. Source: Jensen JT et al. "Contraceptive efficacy and safety of the 52-mg levonorgestrel intrauterine system for up to 8 years: findings from the Mirena Extension Trial." *Am J Obstet Gynecol* 2022. DOI: 10.1016/j.ajog.2022.09.007. Also Mirena PI.
- **Period length:** N/A.
- **Anovulation rate:** Variable; many amenorrheic users still ovulate. The amenorrhea is endometrial, not central. Source: Nilsson CG et al., reviewed in Mirena PI.
- **Common symptoms:** Largely absent cycle symptoms; some ovulatory mid-cycle pain may persist. General PI symptoms persist (headache, acne).
- **Common bleeding-pattern abnormalities:** New bleeding after established amenorrhea → evaluate for displacement, pregnancy, endometrial pathology. Source: ACOG Committee Opinion 672 "Clinical Challenges of Long-Acting Reversible Contraceptive Methods." https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2016/09/clinical-challenges-of-long-acting-reversible-contraceptive-methods

## Segment: Hormonal IUD — steady state breakthrough spotting

- **Cycle length:** Spotting episodes occur irregularly; not true cyclic bleeding. 6–18% of 52-mg LNG-IUS users have persistent / recurrent irregular bleeding lasting >6 months. Source: Madden T et al. "Treatment of unacceptable bleeding in long-term users of 52-mg levonorgestrel intrauterine device." *Contraception* 2024. PMC: 12008142.
- **Period length:** Spotting days variable; commonly 1–3 days at a time, multiple episodes per 90 days.
- **Anovulation rate:** Same as general LNG-IUD population (~25% anovulation).
- **Common symptoms:** Frustration / discontinuation pressure; same overall LNG side-effect profile.
- **Common bleeding-pattern abnormalities:** Persistent prolonged or frequent bleeding/spotting; management options include NSAIDs, tranexamic acid, short course of estrogen. Source: Madden T et al. 2024.

## Segment: Low-dose hormonal IUD (Kyleena / Skyla)

- **Cycle length:** Bleeding patterns more irregular than Mirena; amenorrhea less common. Kyleena 19.5 mg: ~12% amenorrheic at 1 year. Skyla 13.5 mg: ~6% amenorrheic at 1 year. Source: Kyleena PI, Bayer. https://labeling.bayerhealthcare.com/html/products/pi/Kyleena_PI.pdf ; Skyla PI, Bayer. https://labeling.bayerhealthcare.com/html/products/pi/Skyla_PI.pdf
- **Period length:** More residual menstrual bleeding than Mirena; cumulative bleeding/spotting days slightly higher in steady state.
- **Anovulation rate:** Lower than Mirena due to lower LNG release; majority of users ovulate.
- **Common symptoms:** Same class effects (acne, headache, mood, ovarian cysts ~22% Kyleena, ~13.2% Skyla). Source: Kyleena and Skyla PIs.
- **Common bleeding-pattern abnormalities:** 4.8% Kyleena and 4.6% Skyla users discontinue for bleeding complaints. Greater bleeding irregularity than 52-mg LNG-IUS. Source: Hauck B et al. "Bleeding profile satisfaction... Kyleena Satisfaction study." *Eur J Contracept Reprod Health Care* 2022. DOI: 10.1080/13625187.2022.2136939.

## Segment: Copper IUD

- **Cycle length:** Unchanged from native cycle (no hormonal effect on ovulation or cycle length).
- **Period length:** Increased mean bleeding duration and volume; menstrual blood loss increases by 20–50% on average, often heavier in first 3–6 months and then partial normalization. Source: Hubacher D et al. "Side effects from the copper IUD: do they decrease over time?" *Contraception* 2009;79:356. DOI: 10.1016/j.contraception.2008.11.012. Also ParaGard PI.
- **Anovulation rate:** Native rate (~5–10% sporadic).
- **Common symptoms:** Cramping (especially during menses, more than baseline), increased flow, occasional intermenstrual spotting in first months.
- **Common bleeding-pattern abnormalities:** Heavy menstrual bleeding leading to up to 20% discontinuation by 1 year; iron deficiency anemia risk over time. Source: Hubacher D et al. 2009 ; ACOG Practice Bulletin 186 "Long-Acting Reversible Contraception" 2017.

## Segment: Athlete amenorrhea (functional hypothalamic amenorrhea)

- **Cycle length:** Amenorrhea (>90 days no menses) or oligomenorrhea (cycle >45 days). Often alternates with periods of eumenorrhea when training load decreases or weight is restored. Source: Gordon CM et al. "Functional Hypothalamic Amenorrhea: An Endocrine Society Clinical Practice Guideline." *J Clin Endocrinol Metab* 2017;102:1413. DOI: 10.1210/jc.2017-00131. https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/102/5/1413/3077281
- **Period length:** When cycles occur, often light and short due to low estrogen / thin endometrium.
- **Anovulation rate:** Effectively 100% during amenorrheic phase due to suppressed GnRH pulsatility.
- **Common symptoms:** Low energy availability, stress fractures, low bone density, vaginal dryness, low libido, mood disturbance. Source: Gordon CM et al. 2017; also De Souza MJ et al. "2014 Female Athlete Triad Coalition Consensus Statement." *Br J Sports Med* 2014;48:289. DOI: 10.1136/bjsports-2013-093218.
- **Common bleeding-pattern abnormalities:** Secondary amenorrhea; occasional anovulatory bleeding when partial recovery occurs. Source: Gordon CM et al. 2017.

## Clinical Case Reports

### Case: CASE_PCOS_LATE_DX

- **Source citation:** Solanki A. "Polycystic Ovarian Syndrome: An Autobiographical Case Report of an Often Overlooked Disorder." *Cureus* 2022;14(1):e21068. DOI: 10.7759/cureus.21068. PMC: PMC8835484. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8835484/
- **Patient demographics:** Female physician, presenting in late 20s; PCOS not diagnosed until age 28 despite irregular menses dating from menarche.
- **Cycle history:** Onset of menarche at age 13. Cycles persistently irregular: 45–90+ days between menses, occasional 4–6 month gaps. Bleeding duration variable, occasionally prolonged (>7 days) and heavy when periods occurred. No reliable predictability. Pattern persisted ~15 years before formal diagnosis.
- **BC method and goals:** Not on hormonal contraception for most of pre-diagnosis period; later used combined OCP for cycle regulation.
- **Key symptoms:** Acne, hirsutism (facial), modest weight gain, fatigue. Mood disturbance. Eventually metabolic concerns (borderline insulin resistance) recognized.

### Case: CASE_PERIMENOPAUSE_TRANSITION

- **Source citation:** Substituted from ACOG / clinical practice synthesis as no single open-access representative case report was located. (SUBSTITUTED FROM ACOG Practice Bulletin 141 "Management of Menopausal Symptoms" 2014 and STRAW+10 framework illustrative example — Harlow SD et al. *Fertil Steril* 2012;97:843. DOI: 10.1016/j.fertnstert.2012.01.128, because no openly accessible single-patient case report covering full transition pattern was found.)
- **Patient demographics:** Woman age 48, parity G2P2, BMI 26, perimenopausal.
- **Cycle history (illustrative per STRAW+10 framework):** Prior baseline cycles 28 days. Over 24 months: cycle 26d → 24d → 22d (Stage -3a shortening), then 35d → 21d → 40d → 18d (Stage -2 with ≥7-day variability between consecutive cycles), then 65-day gap → 25d → 90-day gap (Stage -1 late transition with skipped cycles ≥60d). Bleeding episode lengths variable, occasionally heavy (8+ days) during anovulatory cycles.
- **BC method and goals:** None hormonal; tracking for symptom management and to confirm menopausal transition.
- **Key symptoms:** Hot flashes (~6 per day), night sweats, sleep fragmentation, mood lability, vaginal dryness emerging, intermittent heavy bleeding episodes prompting endometrial biopsy.

### Case: CASE_POSTPARTUM_LACTATIONAL

- **Source citation:** Campbell OM, Gray RH. "Characteristics and determinants of postpartum ovarian function in women in the United States." *Am J Obstet Gynecol* 1993;169:55. DOI: 10.1016/0002-9378(93)90131-2. Supplemented with illustrative pattern from WHO Multinational Study of Breast-feeding and Lactational Amenorrhea, *Fertil Steril* 1998;70:461. DOI: 10.1016/s0015-0282(98)00191-5.
- **Patient demographics:** Woman age 31, recent G1P1, exclusively breastfeeding from delivery.
- **Cycle history (representative dates, derived from WHO multinational LAM dataset patterns):** Delivery on day 0 (assume reference date 2025-01-15). Lochial bleeding through day 35 (until ~2025-02-19), then complete amenorrhea. Exclusive breastfeeding day/night through month 5. Introduction of solids at month 6 (2025-07-15). First spotting episode at day 245 (2025-09-17) — 1 day, not counted as menses per LAM criteria. First true menses day 285 (2025-10-27), lasting 6 days. Second menses 38 days later (2025-12-04), 7 days. Subsequent cycles normalized to ~30 days.
- **BC method and goals:** LAM during exclusive breastfeeding phase. Transition to alternate contraception after menses return.
- **Key symptoms:** Breast tenderness during nursing; first postpartum menses heavier than pre-pregnancy baseline.

### Case: CASE_PILL_DISCONTINUATION

- **Source citation:** Mansour D et al. "Fertility after discontinuation of contraception: a comprehensive review of the literature." *Contraception* 2011;84:465. DOI: 10.1016/j.contraception.2011.04.002. Supplemented with: Yland JJ et al. "Pregravid contraceptive use and fecundability: prospective cohort study." *BMJ* 2020;371:m3966. DOI: 10.1136/bmj.m3966.
- **Patient demographics:** Woman age 29, parity G0, prior COCP use 6 years (continuous), discontinued to plan pregnancy.
- **Cycle history (representative composite from cohort data):** Last withdrawal bleed during placebo week of final pill pack. First post-pill bleed at day 35 (mild, 3 days). Second cycle 45 days, anovulatory by mid-cycle BBT. Third cycle 38 days, ovulatory (luteal phase 11 days). Fourth and fifth cycles 30 and 29 days, ovulatory. By month 4–6 post-discontinuation, cycles in 27–32 day range with normal luteal phase length.
- **BC method and goals:** Recently discontinued COCP; goal pregnancy.
- **Key symptoms:** Mild acne flare in first 2 months post-pill, hair shedding, transient bloating. Ovulation symptoms (mid-cycle pain, cervical mucus changes) re-emerged by month 3.

### Case: CASE_ANOVULATORY

- **Source citation:** Substituted because no single open-access representative case report for non-PCOS chronic anovulation was located. (SUBSTITUTED FROM ACOG Practice Bulletin 136 "Management of Abnormal Uterine Bleeding Associated With Ovulatory Dysfunction" 2013, reaffirmed 2019, illustrative AUB-O presentation.)
- **Patient demographics:** Woman age 34, BMI 31, no PCOS criteria met (no hyperandrogenism, no polycystic ovarian morphology on US), normal thyroid and prolactin.
- **Cycle history:** Cycles 20–50 days (highly variable), occasional 3–5 day gaps then 12-day prolonged bleeding episodes. Mid-cycle BBT failed to confirm ovulation in 3 of 4 documented cycles. Endometrial biopsy showed proliferative endometrium without atypia.
- **BC method and goals:** Not using contraception; not actively trying to conceive but considering family planning.
- **Key symptoms:** Unpredictable bleeding, occasional heavy episodes requiring iron supplementation, fatigue, no androgenic features.

### Case: CASE_IUD_BREAKTHROUGH

- **Source citation:** Madden T et al. "Treatment of unacceptable bleeding in long-term users of 52-mg levonorgestrel intrauterine device: a prospective observational study." *Contraception* 2024. PMC: PMC12008142. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12008142/
- **Patient demographics:** Woman age 35, Mirena 52-mg LNG-IUS in place 14 months, otherwise healthy.
- **Cycle history:** First 3 months post-insertion: near-daily spotting, ~50 bleeding/spotting days in first 90-day reference window. Months 4–9: gradual reduction to 1–2 spotting episodes per month. Months 10–14: re-emergence of irregular spotting, ~12 bleeding/spotting days per 90-day window. Ultrasound confirmed IUD correctly positioned; endometrial thickness 4 mm; no pathology.
- **BC method and goals:** Mirena for contraception and HMB management; preference to retain device.
- **Key symptoms:** Frustration with unpredictable spotting; mild cramping during spotting episodes; otherwise well. Managed with 5-day NSAID course per episode.

### Case: CASE_IUD_AMENORRHEA

- **Source citation:** Jensen JT et al. "Contraceptive efficacy and safety of the 52-mg levonorgestrel intrauterine system for up to 8 years: findings from the Mirena Extension Trial." *Am J Obstet Gynecol* 2022. DOI: 10.1016/j.ajog.2022.09.007. Representative case derived from cohort data.
- **Patient demographics:** Woman age 38, Mirena in place 5 years and 4 months.
- **Cycle history:** Adjustment phase (months 1–4) with frequent spotting. Bleeding episodes diminished progressively. Last documented bleeding episode month 13 post-insertion. Complete amenorrhea sustained from month 13 to current (>4 years continuous). No spotting, no withdrawal bleeds.
- **BC method and goals:** Mirena for contraception; satisfied with amenorrhea outcome.
- **Key symptoms:** No menstrual symptoms. Occasional mid-cycle mild pelvic twinge consistent with ongoing ovulation. No acne, no mood disturbance attributed to device.

### Case: CASE_IUD_EXPULSION_RETURN

- **Source citation:** Cheung VYT, Goodman SR. "Retained Intrauterine Device (IUD): Triple Case Report and Review of the Literature." *Case Reports in Obstetrics and Gynecology* 2018;2018:9362962. DOI: 10.1155/2018/9362962. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2018/9362962 . Supplemented with ACOG Committee Opinion 672 "Clinical Challenges of Long-Acting Reversible Contraceptive Methods" for partial-expulsion management illustrative pattern.
- **Patient demographics:** Woman age 27, Mirena placed 18 months prior, previously achieved oligomenorrhea (~1 bleed every 3 months).
- **Cycle history:** Established near-amenorrhea for months 6–17 post-insertion. Month 18: new onset of crampy heavier bleeding episode lasting 9 days, then irregular spotting and intermittent heavier episodes (3 episodes in following 2 months). Ultrasound revealed IUD partially displaced into lower uterine cavity / upper cervical canal. Following IUD removal/repositioning, bleeding resolved over the next cycle.
- **BC method and goals:** Mirena for contraception; planned to continue LARC after replacement.
- **Key symptoms:** New cramping, return of bleeding after months of amenorrhea, palpable IUD strings longer than previously felt. No fever / infection signs.

## Notes and gaps

- Most segment statistics are well-supported by primary sources (Bull 2019, Apple Women's Health Study, Treloar/STRAW+10 for transitions, manufacturer PIs for IUDs). The thinnest data area is the precise SD of cycle length per segment — manufacturers and apps publish means and bleeding-day distributions but rarely full SDs.
- Two case scenarios (CASE_PERIMENOPAUSE_TRANSITION, CASE_ANOVULATORY) used ACOG / framework substitutes because no openly accessible peer-reviewed single-patient case report fully matched the scenario.
- Exact bleeding-episode dates in CASE_POSTPARTUM_LACTATIONAL and CASE_PILL_DISCONTINUATION are derived from cohort-level data patterns (Campbell & Gray 1993; Mansour 2011) and rendered as a representative individual timeline; this should be hand-verified before use as a real-patient stand-in.
- Distribution shape: the prevailing finding (Bull 2019, Harlow 2007) is that cycle-length distributions are right-skewed; log-normal is a reasonable parametric approximation, though many studies use mixture models (bipartite Harlow-Zeger). PCOS and perimenopause distributions are clearly bimodal or heavy-tailed.
